For a print book, Wordsmith Book lays out every page and writes a PDF that a print-on-demand
service or a printer can use as the book's interior. The cover is a separate file you make
elsewhere.

<Callout type="warn" title="Built, not in the window yet">
  Typesetting the whole book and writing the print PDF work inside Book today and are tested on
  full-length novels. You can't export the PDF from the window yet: that comes with the print
  preview and export steps. <Status value="in-progress" />
</Callout>

## What Book does on every page

- **Justified text with hyphenation** for the book's language, with no more than two hyphens in a
  row.
- **No widows or orphans.** A page never starts or ends with a single line of a paragraph.
- **Mirrored margins** with a wider inside margin (the gutter) so text doesn't disappear into the
  binding.
- **Chapters start on a right-hand page** when the design says so, with a blank page before them
  if needed.
- **Page numbers** in lower-case roman numerals (i, ii, iii) in the front matter, then 1, 2, 3 from
  the first chapter or part. Chapter openings get their page number at the foot, and blank pages
  have none.
- **Running heads** at the top of the page, as the design sets them: author and title, or title and
  chapter. Never on chapter openings, part pages or blank pages.
- **Chapter openings** from the design: set a little way down the page, with drop caps going down
  two to four lines, or the first words in small capitals.
- **Scene breaks** as an ornament, asterisks or a blank line, and never an indent on the first line
  after one.
- **Part pages** alone on a right-hand page, with the part's number and title.
- **A contents page** with dot leaders and the right page numbers.
- **Real small capitals**, verse that keeps its line breaks, block quotes and pictures.
- **Notes** numbered in each chapter and printed at the chapter's end.

Text that only looks like markup, such as `<b>` or `*stars*`, prints exactly as you typed it.

## Trim sizes

The trim is the size of the finished page. Book has four common trade paperback sizes today:

- 5 × 8 in
- 5.25 × 8 in
- 5.5 × 8.5 in
- 6 × 9 in

More sizes, including metric ones and hardcover sizes, are planned. <Status value="planned" />

## Margins that grow with the book

A thicker book needs a wider gutter. Book sets the inside margin from the page count, following
the common print-on-demand table:

| Pages | Inside margin |
| --- | --- |
| Up to 150 | 0.375 in |
| 151 to 300 | 0.5 in |
| 301 to 500 | 0.625 in |
| 501 to 700 | 0.75 in |
| Over 700 | 0.875 in |

Because the margin changes the page count, and the page count changes the margin, Book lays the
book out, counts the pages, sets the gutter and lays it out again until everything settles. Outside
margins never go below 0.25 in.

## Paper

Each print edition has white, cream or color paper. Book knows the thickness of each per page, to
work out the spine width for the cover template when that arrives.

## The print PDF

The PDF Book writes is what print services ask for:

- single pages, not spreads, at the exact trim size
- every font embedded in the file
- no crop marks, links, bookmarks, comments or form fields

Building the same book twice gives exactly the same file, byte for byte. In testing, every
letter of the printed text is read back out of the PDF and checked against the source.

## Print profiles

A **print profile** is the set of rules for where the book will be printed. Book has two:

- **Print-on-demand paperback**: the trims and margins above, 24 to 828 pages on white paper
  (776 on cream), an even page count, no more than two blank pages in a row inside the book, and
  nothing printed smaller than 7 pt.
- **Generic PDF**: any trim, no page limits, fonts embedded and nothing extra in the file. Good for
  a local printer or a PDF to read.
