For the ebook, Wordsmith Book writes an EPUB 3 file: the standard format that ebook stores and
reading apps take. It comes from the same book and the same design as the print edition, so the
two match.

## What's in the EPUB

- **One file per chapter or page**, marked up so reading apps know what each one is: title page,
  copyright page, dedication, chapter, part and so on.
- **A table of contents** that reading apps show in their menu, with parts holding their chapters.
  Book also adds the older form of contents that older e-readers use.
- **Landmarks**, so a reader can jump to the cover, the contents or the start of the story.
- **Notes** linked both ways: tap the number to read the note at the end of the chapter, and tap
  again to go back.
- **Pictures** from your manuscript, and the right language marked on every page and on any
  passage in another language.
- **A style sheet from your design**: the chapter openings with a drop cap or small-capital lead-in
  as the design says. It is kept simple, so readers can still change the font and text size in
  their reading app.

The ebook leaves out the half title, which only makes sense in print. The contents page is the
ebook's own navigation, placed where the contents stand in your book.

## The ebook's identity

If the ebook edition has an ISBN, it becomes the ebook's identifier. If not, Book makes a stable
identifier from the book, so the same book always gets the same one.

Building the same book twice gives exactly the same file, byte for byte.

## Accessibility

Accessible ebooks are a legal requirement in some markets, and they are better for every reader.
Book writes the accessibility information stores ask for, worked out from what the book holds:

- how the book can be read (as text, plus pictures if it has any)
- its features, such as a table of contents, structural navigation and a reading order
- that it has no hazards such as flashing content
- a short accessibility summary, and the standard it aims for

**Every picture needs a short description** (alternative text) for readers who can't see it. A
picture without one blocks the ebook until you add one, or mark the picture as decorative.
Decorative pictures, such as an ornament, are hidden from screen readers.

## Fonts

Each ebook edition can embed the design's fonts, so the book looks as designed in reading apps
that use them, or leave them out and let the reader's app choose. Book only embeds fonts whose
license allows it.

## The cover

The ebook edition names its cover picture, a PNG or JPEG in the book project. Book puts it first
in the ebook, marks it as the cover for stores and reading apps, and checks its size, file size
and shape against what stores usually ask for: at least 1,600 pixels on the long side, no more than
4 MB, and about 1.6 times as tall as it is wide.

Book doesn't design covers. You make the cover art elsewhere and bring in the picture.
