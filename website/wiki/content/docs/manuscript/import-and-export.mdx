Every import and every export in Manuscript is **checked word for word**. An import whose words
don't all arrive, in order, is not made. An export is read back and checked before the file is
kept. Neither ever changes the words of your project.

## Importing

File › Import brings documents into the open project. Each import shows a **preview** first (the
binder it will make) and a **report**: what was made, what formatting couldn't be kept, and the
result of the word-for-word check.

### Documents

Manuscript imports **DOCX** (Word), **ODT** (OpenDocument), **RTF**, **Markdown**, **HTML** (web
pages), **EPUB** and **plain text**.

- **Headings** become parts, chapters and scenes. With three heading levels: part, chapter and
  scene. With two: part and chapter. With one: chapter.
- A line on its own like **"Chapter 12"**, "CHAPTER XII." or "Part Two" starts a chapter or part
  even without a heading style. You can switch this rule off.
- **Scene breaks** (a line of `***`, `* * *`, `#`, a run of one symbol, a short centered symbol
  line, or a mark you choose) split a chapter into scenes, or stay as scene breaks.
- Italic, bold, small caps, strikethrough, text in another language, notes and comments are kept.
  Underlined text becomes italic, or is left plain. Fonts, sizes and colors are dropped, and the
  report lists them.
- **Plain text**: hard-wrapped lines are joined into paragraphs (or every line is kept), and text
  with no chapters is split at separator lines.
- **Web pages**, such as a draft or a serial's chapter saved from a browser: when the page marks
  its main text, only that is imported, without menus, headers or footers.
- **EPUB books** come back as an editable manuscript, one chapter per reading page.

### Outlines

An **OPML** file from an outliner becomes binder items. Each outline line becomes an item, and its
note becomes the item's synopsis. Lines with lines under them become folders. A title like "Part
Two", "Book I", "Act One" or "Chapter 12" sets the role; otherwise the shape does (the innermost
lines are scenes, the ones above them chapters, anything above that parts).

### A set of documents

**Document Set (a Folder)** imports every document in a folder, placed by what its name says:

| Name contains | Goes to |
| --- | --- |
| (Plan), (Outline) | Notes |
| (Rough Work), (Notes), (Research) | Research |
| (First Draft), (Second Draft), (Third Draft), (Final Draft), (Draft), or nothing | The manuscript |

Several files of one draft ("Part 1", "Part 2", or numbered names) are joined in name order. When
the set holds several drafts, the latest is the one you write and the earlier ones are kept as
[drafts](./drafts-and-progress.mdx#drafts), matched chapter by chapter so you can compare them.

### Notes for the story bible

A folder of Markdown notes can become story bible entries. See
[The story bible](./story-bible.mdx#importing-a-folder-of-notes).

## Exporting

File › Export (<Kbd>Mod</Kbd>+<Kbd>E</Kbd>) writes the manuscript to a file.

| Format | For |
| --- | --- |
| Standard manuscript (DOCX, PDF) | Agents, editors and publishers |
| Word processor (DOCX), OpenDocument (ODT), rich text (RTF) | Other word processors |
| Markdown, plain text, web page (HTML) | The web and other tools |
| Reading copy (EPUB) | Reading your draft on a phone or e-reader |
| Book package (`.wsmz`) | Wordsmith Book (see [Sending to Wordsmith Book](./sending-to-book.mdx)) |

### Options

- **Which items**: by default, every manuscript item that is included in export.
- **Chapter and part titles**: their own titles, "Chapter 1", "Chapter One", "One", "Chapter 1:
  Title", or none.
- **Scene titles** as headings, or not.
- **The scene-break symbol.**
- **Front and back matter**, in or out.
- **Replace-text rules.**
- **END** after the last line.
- **Comments as review comments**, for DOCX, ODT and RTF.

You can save your options as a preset to use again.

### The standard manuscript

This is the format agents, editors and publishers expect:

- Letter or A4 paper, 1-inch margins, 12-point serif or monospaced type, double spacing,
  half-inch indents.
- A title page with your name and contact lines, the word count rounded ("about 82,000 words"),
  the title a third of the way down, and the name you publish under.
- From the first page of text, a header with your surname, the short title and the page number.
- Each chapter starts on a new page, a third of the way down. Scene breaks are a centered `#`.
  The text ends with END.

Set your name, pen name, surname, short title and contact lines in File › Book Details. View ›
[Pages](./writing.mdx#pages) shows the pages before you export.

### The reading copy

The EPUB reading copy is for reading your own draft on a phone or e-reader. Each part and chapter
starts its own page with a contents entry, and notes are numbered at the end of their chapter. It
is not a finished ebook; for that, use Wordsmith Book.

### Other formats, through a converter

For a format Manuscript doesn't write, you can use a document converter you have installed
yourself (Manuscript never comes with one). On File › Export, choose **Another format, through a
converter you installed**. Give the converter program and its arguments, with `{input}` where it
takes the manuscript (Manuscript hands it a DOCX), `{output}` where it writes the file, and
`{format}` for the file's extension.

The program runs on your computer only, and is stopped if it runs for more than five minutes.
When Manuscript can read the result back it checks every word; otherwise the report says it was
not checked.

## The command line

Manuscript also comes with a command-line tool, `manuscript-cli`, that can convert, export,
count and check projects without opening the window. For example, to turn a Word document into a
project:

```sh
cargo run --release -p wordsmith-manuscript-cli -- convert draft.docx novel.wsm
```

Run it with `help` to see every command.
