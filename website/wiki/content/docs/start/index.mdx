Wordsmith is three apps, one for each kind of writing. They share a look, the same keys for the
same things, and the same promises: free, open source, no account and no tracking.

## Which app is for me?

<Cards>
  <Card title="Wordsmith Script" href="../script/index.mdx" icon={<AppIcon app="wordsmith-script" />}>
    For screenplays, TV episodes and stage plays. It keeps the industry page format for you,
    and has what a production needs: revision colors, locked pages, scene numbers and reports.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Wordsmith Manuscript" href="../manuscript/index.mdx" icon={<AppIcon app="wordsmith-manuscript" />}>
    For novels and other long work. Write in scenes, plan on a corkboard or outline, keep a story
    bible of your characters and places, and export a standard manuscript.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Wordsmith Book" href="../book/index.mdx" icon={<AppIcon app="wordsmith-book" />}>
    For the last step: turning a finished manuscript into a typeset print book and an ebook,
    with a design, front and back matter, and checks before you export.
  </Card>
</Cards>

A screenwriter may only ever need Script. A novelist will usually write in Manuscript and, when the
book is done, take it into Book. Book also takes a manuscript written anywhere else, as a Word,
OpenDocument, RTF, Markdown, plain text or EPUB file.

## How they fit together

- **Script and Manuscript:** a Manuscript project can keep Script files alongside your chapters,
  for example a screenplay adaptation. Manuscript shows them read-only and opens them in Script
  when you want to edit.
- **Manuscript to Book:** Book reads your Manuscript project as it is and never changes it. If
  you fix a typo in Book, the fix is offered back, and you accept or reject it in Manuscript.

[Moving between the apps](./moving-between-apps.mdx) has the details.

The Wordsmith Launcher, one small app to install the three, keep them up to date and open your
recent work, is <Status value="planned" />. It is not started.
