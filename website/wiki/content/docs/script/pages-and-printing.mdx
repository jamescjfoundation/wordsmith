## How pages are laid out

Pages follow the screenplay convention: US Letter paper, Courier Prime at 12
point, a 1.5 inch left margin, and the page number at the top right with a
period, with no number on page 1. You don't have to do anything to get this.

- A speech that breaks across pages gets **(MORE)** at the foot of the page, and
  the character's name again with **(CONT'D)** at the top of the next one.
- A character who speaks again after some action in the same scene gets
  **(CONT'D)** too.
- A scene heading is never left alone at the foot of a page, and action and
  dialogue break only after a sentence.
- **Scene CONTINUEDs** (CONTINUED: at the top and foot of a page in the middle
  of a scene) can be switched on.

To switch CONTINUEDs on, or change any of these texts, search for **More and
Continued Settings** in the command search box
(<Kbd>Mod</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>P</Kbd>).

The [page view](./views.mdx#views-for-writing) shows the pages exactly as they
will print.

<Callout type="info">
Pages are US Letter only for now. A4 paper is not available yet.
</Callout>

## Title page

**Title Page** on the **Insert** tab edits the title page: title, credit,
authors, source, notes, contact, draft date and copyright. It prints before the
script, which is then numbered from page 1.

## Headers and footers

**Header & Footer** on the **Insert** tab sets left, center and right text for
the header and for the footer. These fields are filled in on every page:

| Field | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| `{page}` | the page number |
| `{scene}` | the scene heading on that page |
| `{date}` | today's date, or a date you set |
| `{title}` | the script's title |
| `{revision}` | the current revision set's name |
| `{collated}` | every revision set used in the script |

You can also choose whether page 1 shows them, the first page number, and a
footer on page 1 that differs from the header.

## Watermark

**Watermark** on the **Insert** tab prints text across every page, at the
strength you choose, with the name of who the copy is for. It's useful for
numbered or named copies of a script you send out.

## Export a PDF

**Export PDF** (<Kbd>Mod</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>E</Kbd>, or **Export** on
the File page) saves exactly what the page view shows. The text in the PDF can
be selected and searched. **Revised Pages as PDF** saves only the pages with
revisions (see [Production](./production.mdx)).

## Print

- **Print** (<Kbd>Mod</Kbd>+<Kbd>P</Kbd>) prints the script as the page view
  shows it.
- **Print…** (<Kbd>Mod</Kbd>+<Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>Shift</Kbd>+<Kbd>P</Kbd>, or
  **Print** on the File page) lets you choose:
  - everything, a range of pages, or a range of scenes;
  - revised pages, unrevised pages, or one revision set;
  - the number of copies (collated), and last page first;
  - whether to include the title page and the revision marks;
  - **sides** for a character: only the scenes they are in, with the scene
    numbers they have in the full script and their name on every page.
- **Print Preview** (<Kbd>Mod</Kbd>+<Kbd>Alt</Kbd>+<Kbd>P</Kbd>) opens the pages
  in your PDF viewer.

<Tabs items={['macOS', 'Windows', 'Linux']}>
  <Tab value="macOS">Printing goes through the system's print service.</Tab>
  <Tab value="Windows">The PDF opens in your PDF viewer, and you print from there.</Tab>
  <Tab value="Linux">Printing goes through the system's print service (CUPS).</Tab>
</Tabs>

Notes are never printed. Outline elements print only while **Show Outline** is
on (see [Planning](./planning.mdx#outline-elements-in-the-script)).
